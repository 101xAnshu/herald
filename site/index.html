Herald accepts a business event, commits it with an outbox row, resolves policy per recipient, splits fanout by priority, and carries every delivery to a traceable terminal state. No lost events, no silent retries, no dashboard required to see the truth.

One durable path

Accept first. Decide later.

Ingest does one thing: resolve the recipient, check idempotency, and commit the notification, timeline event, and outbox row together. Everything expensive happens after the producer gets a 202.

Priority is topology, not a promise

Critical, high, normal, and low work each get their own Kafka topic and consumer group. A campaign cannot starve a security alert because they do not share a queue.

Redis accelerates. Postgres decides.

Dedupe, rate limits, and token buckets live in Redis. If Redis disappears, limits reset and dedupe falls back to the unique constraint. Accepted work is still safe.

What is actually built

Small surface. Real failure paths.

Transactional outbox

A committed notification is never acknowledged before its publish intent is durable. The relay replays unpublished rows after a crash or a database promotion.

Policy snapshots

Every fanout decision stores the policy hash, the channels considered, and the reason each one was allowed or suppressed. Weeks later the trace still explains itself.

Retries with full jitter

30 second base, 30 minute cap, per-channel attempt budgets. Permanent errors go straight to failure. Ambiguous timeouts become unknown, not blind retries.

Campaigns on the same pipeline

The runner calls the ingest service, not a shortcut. Pause stops the next batch. Resume continues from the next pending recipient. The funnel is derived, not guessed.

Durable inbox and SSE

In-app delivery commits the inbox row and the delivery state together. Connected clients get a live event; offline clients get the same row on reconnect.

Narrow failover drill

Streaming replication, standby promotion, application restart, and outbox replay. It proves the data plane and says plainly what it does not prove.

Local, not mocked

One command boots the real path.

Postgres, Redis, Kafka, Mailpit, the API, and the worker. Six partitions per priority topic. A seeded 2,200-user Marlowe dataset. A campaign that pauses at 400 recipients and resumes.

  • Email arrives in Mailpit over SMTP.
  • In-app events stream over SSE.
  • Push and SMS use deterministic mock outcomes.
  • herald trace follows one notification end to end.
